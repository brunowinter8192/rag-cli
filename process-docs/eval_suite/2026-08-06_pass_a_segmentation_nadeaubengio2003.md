R3b bans deriving blocks from `#`/`##` structure. Content judgment was done by reading the
full document via `Read` (all 915 lines, multiple calls). Exact blank-line positions were

## Structural discovery: image ref + caption share one paragraph (no legal cut between them)

In this document's PDF-to-markdown conversion, a figure's `![](images/...)` line and its
`Figure N. ...` caption are joined by a markdown hard-break (`  ` trailing double-space, no
blank line) rather than a blank line — e.g. lines 636-637 (Figure 1), 717-718 (Figure 10).
R1 permits boundaries only at blank lines, so these pairs cannot be split into separate
block/trash spans; each was kept as one content block. Where a figure instead has several
standalone `![](images/...)` sub-panel lines with blank-line separation and NO caption text
of their own (e.g. lines 709-716 before Figure 10's actual caption at 717-718), those were
merged into one `conversion_residue` trash span since they carry no extractable content.

## Block-granularity principles applied

- **Formula ↔ prose on one object stays one block** (R1's grounding note): e.g. b011
  (63-80) merges five bulleted definitions (S_j, S_j^c, Z_{S_j}, L(j,i), mu_hat_j) with
  formula (5) and its unbiasedness note into one block, because they cumulatively define a
  single object — the CV estimator — not five separate subjects.
- **Named results (Lemma/Proposition/Conjecture) + their full proof are each own blocks**:
  statement and proof were split (e.g. b016 Lemma 1 statement vs b017 Proof of Lemma 1)
  since the proof is a distinct activity from the claim, but never split mid-proof — e.g.
  b027 (Proposition 3's proof, 215-241, 27 lines) and b090 (A.1 Proof of Lemma 2, 797-816,
  20 lines) are each one continuous algebraic argument with no intervening claim to anchor
  a cut, the R3b large-block exception.
- **Simulation tables (Table 2/3/4) are each one block** despite being 28-37 lines: a
  table of per-simulation parameters and confidence intervals is one data object, not a
  sequence of shifting subjects — same-object carve-out, not under-segmentation.
- **Section/subsection headings merge into their immediately following lead sentence**,
  never stand alone: a bare `## N. Title` line carries no independent subject.
