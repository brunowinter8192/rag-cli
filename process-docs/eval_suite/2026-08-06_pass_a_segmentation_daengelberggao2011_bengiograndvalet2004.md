## Method: authoritative paragraph-unit map generated before hand-planning

Rather than manually counting blank lines across 1134 combined lines (high transcription-error
risk at this scale), a throwaway `/tmp` Python script parsed both source `.md` files into
blank-line-delimited paragraph units with exact `(start, end)` line numbers before any
subject-shift judgment was made. Subject-shift decisions were then made against this ground
truth, and the final JSON was generated by a second script from an ordered list of
`(start_line, kind, subject_or_trash_type)` tuples with `end = next_start - 1` — eliminating
manual coverage/overlap arithmetic entirely. Both files validated clean on the first run.

## Mid-sentence page-break blank lines (PDF conversion artifact)

DaEngelbergGao2011 has ~15 instances where a blank line splits one running sentence across a
page break (e.g. L23 "...In February of" / blank / L25 "2009, Google accounted for..."; same
pattern at L29/31, L37/39, L43/45, L65/67, L77/79, L87/89, L107/111, L131/133, L149/151,
L159/161, L165/167, L177/179, L185/189, L195/197, L201/203). Per the prompt's explicit
guidance, these were merged across rather than treated as legal R2 boundaries — each pair
forms one block despite spanning the blank line.

## Formula-dense sections stay one block per logical unit, not one block per formula

BengioGrandvalet2004 alternates prose and `$$...$$` display formulas heavily. Per R1's
grounding note (formula↔prose alternation on one subject stays one block), each Lemma/
Corollary/Theorem statement was kept as one block including its embedded formulas, and its
Proof (when present) as a separate block — never split at every formula's surrounding blank
line. Example: b032 (152-161) merges the general Θ-variance formula, its covariance
definition, and the explanatory sentence into one block, since all three elaborate the same
object. The largest resulting block, b035 "Proof of Lemma1" (178-215, 38 lines), is one
continuous permutation-invariance argument — the R3b same-object large-block exception, not
under-segmentation.

## Caption reclassification: `caption_stub` is not a length-based category

Initial pass classified two long caption spans as monolithic `caption_stub` trash (Doc1
268-291 covering Figures 1-3; Doc2 five figure-caption spans). On review it was flagged that
`caption_stub` is reserved for orphan captions carrying no standalone content — the same
distinction already established in the NadeauBengio2003 pass A run (see that entry in this
area). Corrected:

- **Doc1**: Figure 1's caption spells out the exact SVI-scaling methodology (how "diet"/
  "cranberry" and "MSFT"/"AAPL" search volumes are each normalized against their own vs. a
  shared reference term) — promoted to a content block. Figure 2 and Figure 3 captions state
  the sample period and N=181 IPOs plus what each panel plots — both promoted. The bare
  `Panel A: ...` / `Figure N: ...` one-line labels around them (9 spans, no parameters, no
  standalone content) stayed `caption_stub`.
- **Doc2**: Figure 1's caption names and contrasts the two estimators (Θ1 naive vs. Θ2
  correlation-aware) and the experiment scale (100,000 experiments) — promoted. Figure 3's
  caption gives concrete parameter values (n=200, K={2,5,10,100}) — promoted. Figures 4 and 5
  each specify their x-axis variable and, for Figure 5, a fixed n=120 — both promoted (and
  split into two blocks at their existing blank-line boundary, since they describe two
  distinct sub-experiments). Figure 6's caption also names its axis variable and experiment
  number — promoted for consistency with Figures 4/5. Figure 2's caption ("Structure of the
  covariance matrix.") has zero parameters and no content beyond the figure title — the one
  span that stayed `caption_stub`.

Net effect: Doc1 trash spans grew from 6 to 14 (one `caption_stub` span split into 9 orphan
spans + 3 lines promoted to blocks); Doc2 trash spans shrank from 9 to 5 (4 `caption_stub`
spans promoted to 5 content blocks, one span split in two). Both re-validated clean after the
correction.
